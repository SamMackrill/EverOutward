Ever Outward:
The Next Gate

A shared record of our National Trust visits, with one simple challenge: visit the nearest place we haven’t explored yet.

Product & implementation planNational Trust · England, Wales & Northern IrelandPersonal visit journal

The website’s promise

Open the site and know where to go next. Explore the UK map, see how far from home we’ve reached, and turn each visit into a memory with dates, photo links, notes and a star rating.

From your brief

What the first version must do

  • Set a home or other starting location.
  • Find the nearest unvisited National Trust destination and show the next five in order.
  • Show an interactive UK map with all catalogue locations and a filled, semi-transparent circle for the local visit range.
  • Show the complete visit history in an infinitely scrollable, date-ordered timeline with a selected representative photo, short summary and full visit detail on click.
  • Show distances, travel times and rich mouse-over details: photo, description and opening times.
  • Distinguish visited locations by colour and show our own visit details.
  • Record dates, the people who came along, multiple photo links, descriptions and a 1–5 star rating.
  • Allow named guest comments on published photos and visit text, without login.
  • Create the kissing-gate couple icon at multiple sizes, and use National Trust colours with light/dark mode.

Confirmed scope includes driving-distance ranking, National Trust-only coverage, named guest comments, the complete visit-history timeline, the couple icon and National Trust-based light/dark themes. Confirmed first release: local owner editing with SQLite, public here.now publishing, Waze navigation and free FOSSGIS/OSRM driving estimates, with manual overrides. Comments publish immediately. Exact home coordinates and the circle stay in the local workspace.

The core behaviour

Always start from home

Each new visit removes a place from the queue. The next gate is recalculated from the same home base, rather than from the last place visited.

1

Order the unvisited places

Confirmed: driving distance from home to the verified visitor entrance. Use one consistent car-routing policy for all candidates. Sort on unrounded route metres; break exact ties by stable place ID. Display rounded miles.

2

Keep the next five in view

Number 1 is the next gate. Numbers 2–5 show what comes after it. If fewer remain, show all remaining places; when none remain, celebrate completion.

3

Grow the circle after a visit

The filled, semi-transparent circle reaches the outermost visited place geographically inside the next unvisited destination. Distant, out-of-order visits do not expand this local range. Before the first visit it is zero. The next destination is still selected by driving distance.

Road order, geographic reach

Display driving distance from home and estimated driving time prominently. Label straight-line distance as geographic reach. Route to the verified visitor entrance or car park, which may differ from the map point.

Driving time comes from a routing service and includes its estimate time and travel mode. No route, ferries and unavailable estimates get explicit labels. Do not turn straight-line miles into an invented driving time.

Confirmed circle: geographic metres, ending at the outermost visited location inside the next road-ranked destination. The public circle uses a centre rounded to a 0.1° grid and a radius recalculated from that centre; the exact circle remains owner-only. Show maximum driving distance achieved separately; a road-distance boundary is irregular and cannot be represented faithfully by a circle. A change of home creates a new journey with a new circle; the household’s visit history remains intact.

    Record a visit together

    Enter a visit date, full description, short timeline summary, optional 1–5 star rating, and up to 200 HTTPS photo or album links per visit with captions. Choose one photo to open the full visit carousel; timeline cards use the first available photo. Preview direct image links; show album pages as links. A broken photo keeps its caption and original link.

    Save → show confirmation → update the marker, queue and circle together. Editing or deleting a visit recalculates them too. Returning to an already visited place adds a separate dated journal entry without increasing the distinct-place count. Each visit has its own optional attendee list, displayed in the timeline and full detail; unknown attendees stay blank. Timeline search also matches attendee names.

    What if the nearest place is closed?

    Proposed strict rule: it remains the next gate, with a closure or booking message and an official information link. Looking at the next four does not silently skip it. Any option to defer a destination must be an explicit rule change with a reason; it must never mark the place as visited. Permanently unavailable sites can be retired from the active catalogue after review, with history preserved.

    What about visits made before the challenge?

    Proposed default: existing household visits count as visited and are excluded from the queue. Backdated and out-of-order entries are allowed to keep the journal honest, but an out-of-order entry is labelled as such. That can leave unvisited places inside the circle; the next gate still chooses the nearest one. A challenge-only history is an alternative to decide before implementation.

    Confirmed additional scope · our complete visit history

    Every visit, one unfolding timeline

    Add an infinitely scrollable timeline of the entire current visit history. Each entry shows the visit date, location, an owner-selected representative photo and a short visit summary. Clicking the entry opens the full visit summary.

    A chronological record, including return visits

    Proposed default: newest visits first, with older visits loading as you scroll. Group entries by month and year. Order by the actual visit date, not the date the entry was created; use an immutable visit ID to keep same-day entries in a stable order.

    Show every saved visit in the owner’s household history, including repeat visits, backdated entries and visits from earlier home bases. Do not limit the timeline to the active journey, visible map area or next-five destinations. Removed visits disappear; owners can identify unpublished entries, while guests see published visits only.

    Each entry includes the place name, visit date, first available photo, short summary and any star rating. The photo and title link to the visit, with descriptive accessible names. Use the photo caption or a dedicated alternative description so the card makes sense without the image.

    Choose the memory that leads

    Add “Use as timeline photo” to the visit’s photo editor, plus a summary field in the visit editor. The selected representative photo must belong to that visit. Keep the original image intact; save an optional crop/focal point for its timeline thumbnail.

    If no cover is selected, use the first usable image from the visit. If there is no usable image, show a deliberate “No visit photo yet” treatment alongside the text. Album-only URLs and broken images must not produce an empty card.

    Proposed summary limit: 280 characters. If left blank, use a short plain-text excerpt of the visit description with a clear “Read full visit” link. Editing the summary or cover updates the timeline without changing its date position.

    Use Map and Timeline as two views of the main content area. A selected visit opens a Visit detail view in that same area, replacing the map or timeline temporarily. Keep the site navigation and next-five panel available on desktop. On mobile, use a full-width detail page with an obvious Back control.

    Confirmed additional scope · guest participation

    Let visitors leave a note

    Visitors can comment on our published visit text and on individual photos. No account or login is required. Every submission requires a name and a comment; the name is displayed with the comment.

    Two places to join the conversation

    A “Comments” section follows each visit’s written description. Each photo has its own comments beneath the enlarged image. Show the target clearly so a comment about one photo does not appear under another photo or the whole visit.

    The form has Your name, Your comment and Submit comment. Both fields are required, with whitespace-only values rejected. Proposed limits: 80 characters for the name and 2,000 for the comment. No email field or identity verification is needed; names are self-entered display names.

    Use visible labels and accessible inline errors. Preserve the draft if submission fails, prevent duplicate submissions, and show an explicit success message. Display each published comment’s name, text and date/time in a consistent order.

    Owner controls and publication

    Confirmed: valid named comments appear immediately. The guest sees “Your comment has been posted.” Owners can remove comments through the local workspace. No approval queue or visitor login is required.

    Accept plain text only; escape names and comment bodies. Add server-side length validation, rate limits and a hidden spam field. Only accept submissions for published targets. Unpublishing a visit hides its photos and comments; removing a photo prevents new comments on it.

    Initial scope is a flat comment list. Guest editing, threaded replies, notifications and reactions are possible later additions. The name alone must never grant permission to edit another person’s comment.

    Public memories, private home settings

    Proposed split: visitors read published journal entries and photos and leave named comments; owners sign in to manage visits, publication and moderation. Exact home coordinates and the home-centred circle remain in the owner view. Public responses also omit precise home-to-place distances, which could reveal the origin. A public map can show destination locations without exposing home.

    Confirmed additional scope · visual identity

    A kiss at the next gate

    Create an original Ever Outward icon based on a kissing gate with a couple passing through it and kissing. The woman has dark hair; the man has blonde hair; she is one head shorter than him.

    Composition and character

    Make the gate recognisable as a countryside kissing gate: a hinged gate within a small enclosure, shown partly open. The couple pause in the passage, turning towards one another for a kiss. Keep the height difference readable through their bodies even as he leans down and she tilts her face up.

    Use a simple, warm illustration with clear silhouettes and a restrained green palette. Keep the dark and blonde hair distinct against both light and dark backgrounds. The mark stands on its own without tiny lettering; pair it with the full website title in the header.

Inspect it at actual pixel size rather than relying on a large preview.

    Provide light-background, dark-background and monochrome variants. Use a consistent silhouette, safe padding, clean transparent edges and no baked-in wordmark in square exports. Add accessible text when the icon is the only label for a link.

    Research finding · 20 September 2026

    A real starting point for the places database

    The official National Trust visitor-properties layer returned 643 point records in a complete query. Treat that as a source-record count, not a verified total of current visitor destinations.

    Data neededSource and implementation approachKnown limitation
    Names & coordinatesImport the official visitor-property points. Fields include name, region, Vader ID and GlobalID. Request WGS84 coordinates for the map.The points lie within Trust land; they are not guaranteed visitor entrances.
    Coverage boundaryNational Trust destinations in England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, as confirmed. The UK basemap still includes Scotland.National Trust for Scotland is a separate organisation and is excluded from this catalogue.
    Descriptions & photosLink official pages; write short original summaries. Use our own or explicitly licensed images with credit and rights metadata.The points dataset contains neither. Its data licence does not grant rights to website photography.
    Opening timesUse permitted official information, date-specific hours, separate house/garden access, seasonal exceptions, last entry and booking links.No verified live feed yet. When absent or stale, show “Check official opening times”, not “Open now”.
    Driving estimatesRouting provider using home and verified visitor entrances; cache by origin, destination, mode and departure assumptions.A provider, budget and treatment of ferries still need selecting. A missing route is not zero minutes.

    Import, inspect, then promote

    1. Fetch into staging; record source URL, retrieval date, licence and raw source IDs. Page through results and verify count/transfer-limit flags.
    2. Validate names and coordinates; inspect duplicates, parent estates and separate attractions. Use internal immutable place IDs with source mappings.
    3. Match against current official visitor pages, confirm eligibility and verify entrances. Review differences before activation.
    4. Re-import safely without duplicating places or overwriting our journal. Retire removed places instead of deleting their history.

    What the source establishes

    The item metadata specifies CC BY 4.0 and credits National Trust, SMU, GIS Product Team. The eight simulator points are a transformed subset, with distances calculated for this plan.

    The National Trust’s land-map guidance explains that land records may have multiple acquisitions and restricted public access. Land polygons should not become individual gates.

    This deliverable identifies the import source and requirements. It does not claim to be a reconciled UK catalogue or a populated production database.

    Proposed implementation

    Keep the catalogue and our journal separate

    A responsive web application with public journal pages and guest comments, signed-in household management, a relational database, and a server layer for catalogue imports, routing, visit updates, paginated visit history and comment removal.

    Browser → application server → places + visits + comments database

    The browser displays Map, Timeline and Visit detail views. The server checks owner access, serves published content, accepts named guest comments, saves visits and computes the queue. External services provide map tiles, geocoding and routes; scheduled imports refresh the catalogue.

    Suggested starting stack

    TypeScript with React for the app; PostgreSQL for places and household records; a small server API. Leaflet provides the interactive map, markers and popups. Select a tile provider with suitable usage terms and attribution.

    Compute driving routes to every eligible unvisited destination for the chosen home, cache them, and sort on the server. A straight-line shortlist alone cannot guarantee the nearest five by road. Geodesic calculations separately create the geographic reach polygon at UK-wide scale.

    Published memories and owner-only editing

    Household members share one visit history and sign in to edit it. Guests can read published entries and photos and submit comments with a name, without authentication. Drafts, home settings and moderation tools remain owner-only; public responses must omit exact home coordinates and origin-revealing circle/distance data. Store photo links and captions initially; binary photo uploads can be a later addition.

    Validate links and escape notes. Keep provider keys on the server and protect any server-side image fetch against private-network URLs. Export visits, photo links and comments with their target IDs and moderation status as JSON/CSV; back up the database.

    A complete, stable history feed

    Use cursor pagination ordered by visit date descending and immutable visit ID descending, backed by an index covering household, visibility and that order. Apply owner/public access rules before pagination. Return the next cursor and whether more visits exist; fetch full photo galleries and comment threads when the detail opens.

    Include a history revision with the cursor. If visits are added, backdated, redated, deleted or unpublished during scrolling, invalidate the stale sequence and refresh around the saved visit anchor rather than silently skipping or duplicating records. Recheck publication permissions on every request. Cover-photo and summary edits also invalidate cached cards.

    When routes are incomplete

    Show route-pending or unavailable places explicitly. If any unresolved candidate could change the first five, label the list provisional. Never silently omit unreachable places or switch to straight-line order. Recompute after retries or home changes; use the same route policy for all candidates.

    The queue, maximum road mileage and geographic radius are derived values, not manually maintained flags. Save a visit atomically and then refresh all three from the same data. Repeated clicks or retried requests must not create duplicate entries.

    Confirmed delivery decision · first implementation

    Local owner workspace, published on here.now

    Create a new dedicated, permanent here.now site for Ever Outward and reuse its URL on every publish. The new permanent site is live at quartz-oyster-7zxz.here.now. Dashboard pinning requires the owner to sign in and choose “Pin to top”; the API key cannot perform that dashboard action. Permanent hosting is already active.

    Editing stays on this computer

    React and Leaflet provide the map, timeline and journal. A bundled Natural Earth overview provides reliable map coverage; default OpenStreetMap street detail uses an explicit referrer policy and falls back to the overview on tile errors. A loopback-only Node server and SQLite store the home location, Waze measurements, drafts and owner password hash. First sign-in creates the local owner password. Back up the local data; there is no cloud owner account or device sync in this release.

    Road distance determines visit order. The map circle measures geographic reach and does not claim to represent a road isochrone. Waze directions open in a separate tab. The free FOSSGIS/OSRM service calculates and caches driving distances and times. Public visitors use the published queue; only the local server calculates routes. Estimates follow the service’s fastest-car-route profile, sorted by road distance.

    Publishing shares selected memories

    The local Publish control builds a static website and sends only selected published visits, photo links, catalogue data and the available next-five road queue to here.now. The precise origin, private drafts, account credentials and local distance circle are excluded. The site opens without an owner login.

    Named guest comments publish immediately through here.now Site Data. Owners read and remove those public comments through the local workspace. The API key remains on the local server. Cloud comments are public data once submitted; do not include private information.

    First-release limits and later work

    The official catalogue contains 643 source records, but points still need reconciliation with visitor entrances. Missing descriptions, imagery and opening schedules use honest fallbacks and official information links. No invented opening hours or driving estimates are shown. The current timeline loads the published history snapshot and reveals entries in batches of 20; a database cursor API and large-history virtualisation remain later enhancements. Public comments use platform validation and rate limits; relational target validation and richer spam controls require a dedicated backend.

    Implemented photo workflow and persistence

    Link your photos, keep your memories

    Shared photos and collections

    Open Google Photos, iCloud Photos, Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox or Flickr from the visit editor. Paste or drag in a shared link. Load its public preview, or choose a display image when the provider does not expose one. Drop JPEG, PNG or WebP files into the photo area, or onto an existing link to set its representative image. Any display photo can open the full visit carousel; the timeline uses the first available photo. Full images fit without cropping, including portraits. The header presents a rotating wheel with partially visible, blurred adjacent photos. Play/pause, previous/next, mouse drag, finger swipes and horizontal trackpad gestures control the wheel. Playback defaults to 10 seconds, with an unobtrusive 1–20 second interval selector remembered in local browser storage for each site. It pauses after gestures and starts paused for reduced-motion preferences. Google Photos shared albums can be loaded as a thumbnail selection: paste the album link with the Paste link button, choose the photos to keep, then add them individually. Existing photos are skipped on re-import. Each photo can be removed or chosen as cover without changing the originals. Up to 200 photos can be attached; large albums may expose only part of their contents. Imports are snapshots, not automatic synchronisation. Other providers remain linked collections. Clipboard-blocked browsers focus the link field and show keyboard or touch paste instructions.

    Uploaded display copies are resized and saved in the visit record. Original links remain attached. An ordinary iCloud Link may expire after 30 days; shared albums with public access are preferable for longer-term links.

    Data survives website updates

    Notes, visit records, photo links and uploaded display copies are stored in a private local SQLite database, outside the build directory. Publishing exports selected visits and never reseeds or resets that database. Each publish creates a private database backup, checks for edits made during upload and stops before finalisation if the journal changed. Guest comments remain in here.now Site Data across releases.

    Local editing is not cloud synchronisation: publish to update the public journal. Keep an off-computer backup as well as the automatic local backups. A persistent hosted owner backend is the next step for editing from multiple devices.

    Definition of done

    How we’ll know it works

    The outward rule

    • With a known home and controlled route results, the first five match driving-distance order, including exact ties and a case where road order differs from straight-line order.
    • Saving the next visit removes only that place from the queue; a repeat visit does not alter its distinct-place status.
    • Deleting the final visit to a place restores it to the queue and recomputes the circle, including a shrinking radius if appropriate.
    • No visits means zero radius; fewer than five remaining and all-visited states remain usable.
    • Changing home invalidates route estimates and recalculates the queue and reach without losing history.

    The full experience

    • UK view includes Northern Ireland. All destinations belong to the National Trust catalogue; no National Trust for Scotland sites appear. Clusters expand to individual places.
    • Hover, keyboard focus and touch show the same details; colour is never the only visited indicator.
    • Dates, notes, ratings and multiple photo links persist; broken links, offline map tiles and absent routes have usable fallbacks.
    • Opening times show their date/source; no unverified “open now” claims. Travel time is never a fabricated placeholder.
    • Catalogue refresh preserves visit links. Guests and other households cannot read owner-only home data, drafts or moderation records. Publicly published memories remain readable. Exported records can be restored.

    Acceptance checks · added scope

    Timeline, comments, icon and themes

    • With history spanning multiple pages, every permitted visit appears once in visit-date order, including same-day ties, backdated entries, return visits and visits before a home change. Scrolling reaches the oldest saved visit and stops cleanly.
    • Each timeline card shows its first available photo and summary. The selected cover opens the full visit carousel. Portraits remain fully visible, carousel controls and gestures work, and broken or missing images preserve a usable visit. Only photos from the same visit can be selected.
    • Opening a card shows the full visit, gallery, rating and correct comments. Back and browser Back restore the deep timeline position and focus; direct links and “Show place on map” behave as specified.
    • Adding, redating, deleting or unpublishing visits during pagination triggers a consistent refresh without silent gaps or duplicates. Public responses and direct links never expose unpublished visits.
    • Loading, retry, empty and end-of-history states work on mobile and desktop. The manual load button is keyboard-operable, appended results do not steal focus, and a long history remains responsive.
    • A signed-out guest can comment on a published visit description and a specific photo with only a name and comment. Empty or whitespace-only names/comments are rejected without losing the draft.
    • Comments remain attached to the correct text/photo after photo reordering. Valid named comments appear immediately with a success confirmation; guests cannot edit owner content or comment on drafts. Owners can remove comments.
    • HTML/script payloads in names and comments render as text. Rate limits, duplicate-submit handling and target validation work server-side. Unpublishing hides the associated conversation.
    • The icon clearly shows a couple passing through a kissing gate and kissing, with a dark-haired woman one head shorter than the blonde man. Verify the master, small-size variant, all declared exports and light/dark variants.
    • With no saved choice, the theme follows the device. Light and Dark override it, survive reload where storage is available, and System restores device-following behaviour. Blocked storage does not break the page.
    • Check text and control contrast, map markers, comment forms, popups and image viewers in both themes. All required states remain distinguishable without colour alone.
